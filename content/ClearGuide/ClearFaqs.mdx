<Question>Is it safe?</Question>

<Answer>
"Yes, HPV vaccination is very safe. Like any medication, vaccines can cause side effects, including pain, swelling, or redness where the shot was given. That’s normal for the HPV vaccine too and should go away in a day or two."
</Answer>
</QuestionEntry>


<QuestionEntry>

<Question>What are the side effects?</Question>

<Answer>
"The HPV vaccine, like any vaccine, can cause injection site pain, swelling, or redness. Some adolescents may have a headache or even faint after they get shots, so we recommend the patient stay seated during vaccination."
</Answer>

</QuestionEntry>


<QuestionEntry>

<Question>Why 9 to 12 year-olds?</Question>

<Answer>
"The HPV vaccine works better at younger ages. If your child gets the vaccine today they will only need 2 doses, but if we wait until they are older they may need 3 doses instead."
</Answer>

</QuestionEntry>


<QuestionEntry>

<Question>How well does it work?</Question>

<Answer>
"Clinical trials show that the vaccine is 97-100% effective in preventing HPV cancers. Since the vaccine's release in the US, HPV infection rates have decreased by 89%."
</Answer>

</QuestionEntry>


<QuestionEntry>

<Question>How much does it cost?</Question>

<Answer>
"Most 9-12 year-olds can receive this vaccine free, whether covered by insurance or not."
</Answer>

</QuestionEntry>


<QuestionEntry>

<Question>My child isn't having sex, so why do they need the HPV vaccine now?</Question>

<Answer>
"The HPV vaccine works best when it is given at a younger age because children have a stronger immune response. We also want to protect your child long before they are ever exposed to HPV, which is why we recommend the vaccine now rather than waiting until they are older."
</Answer>

</QuestionEntry>

<QuestionEntry>

<Question>I've heard that the HPV vaccine causes infertility. Is that true?</Question>

<Answer>
"No, the HPV vaccine is completely safe. It has been studied for more than 15 years and over 500 million doses have been given worldwide. There is no evidence that the HPV vaccine causes infertility."
</Answer>
